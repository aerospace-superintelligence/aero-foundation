# Data sources for aero-foundation

This file lists candidate corpora for aero-foundation's two training stages: continued pretraining (CPT), and building evaluation and RL task sets whose rewards come from engineering tools. The target uses are flight control design, aerodynamic databases, handling-qualities analysis, and requirements traceability. The standards context is ARP4754B, DO-178C, DO-330, DO-331 and MIL-STD-1797A.

**Method.** Candidates were found by web search. The official landing page or terms page for each source was opened on **2026-10-15**, and its license or terms of use were read there. Most pages were read with a web fetcher. Where the fetcher was blocked (FAA, CORE, OpenSky, Airfoil Tools, ERAU Pressbooks, NTSB avdata, UIUC FAQ, ASSIST search form), the same official page was read with a plain HTTP GET. **No dataset or document content was downloaded.** Only landing pages, terms pages, catalog records and directory listings were read. Restricted material was not accessed. License wording is paraphrased. Where a fetched page did not state a license, the License column says **"Unverified — check terms"**, even when a license seems likely. A size or count appears only if a fetched page stated it; otherwise the entry says "not stated".

**US government works.** Under 17 U.S.C. §105, works prepared by US federal employees as part of their official duties are not eligible for US copyright. GovInfo's policies page says this, and so do NASA's STI disclaimers. Three things still prevent bulk ingestion of agency repositories as if everything were public domain:

1. **Third-party copyright.** NASA's STI terms say that contractor, grantee and collaborator works can stay under private copyright even when the government holds a license. Putting a private work inside a government work does not make it public domain. NTRS records a copyright status per item, and DTIC stamps show both public-release and "© COPYRIGHTED; U.S. Government or Federal Rights License" markings.
2. **Distribution limits and export control.** DoD repositories mix Distribution A (public release, unlimited) with B–F (restricted). Export-controlled technical data (ITAR/EAR) can appear in any agency repository and in third-party mirrors.
3. **§105 is a US rule.** It does not cover foreign agencies (EASA, ESA, DLR, JAXA), whose reuse terms differ.

So every document needs its own license, distribution and export check before ingestion (see [Filtering and provenance requirements](#filtering-and-provenance-requirements)).

## Filtering and provenance requirements

Every document passes through these gates before it can enter CPT or a task set. A document that fails any gate is excluded, and the exclusion reason is logged.

1. **Per-document license tagging.** Record the license *as stated by the source for that item*. Examples: NTRS `Copyright` field, arXiv license URL from OAI-PMH, OpenAlex `best_oa_location.license`, The Stack v2 `detected_licenses`/`license_type`, CC license on a thesis record. Map it to an SPDX ID or an internal code (`US-GOV-PD`, `EASA-REUSE-ATTRIB`, `CC-BY-4.0`, `UNVERIFIED`, ...). Allowed for training by default: US-Gov public domain, CC0, CC BY, EASA reuse with attribution, and permissive OSS. **Needs a policy decision:** CC BY-SA, GFDL, GPL/LGPL code. **Excluded:** NC, ND, "all rights reserved", arXiv default license, `UNVERIFIED`, `no_license`. Repository-level terms never override the item's own terms.
2. **Distribution statement parsing (DTIC, NTRS, ASSIST).** Parse catalog fields first (NTRS `Distribution Limits`, ASSIST Dist Stmt code and lock flag), then confirm against the document's cover and report-documentation page using OCR and regex for "DISTRIBUTION STATEMENT [A-F]", "Approved for public release", "distribution unlimited", "FEDD" and similar markings. Accept only Distribution A or NTRS "Public", and require the catalog and the document to agree. If they disagree or no marking is found, exclude. Per ASSIST help, Statement F is also used when no statement can be determined.
3. **Export-control screening (ITAR/EAR).** Even when a document is marked public, scan it for export-control legends (ITAR, EAR, "export of this document is restricted", "WARNING – This document contains technical data", ECCN/USML references, "U.S. Release Only"). Also scan for codes listed in the NASA Software Catalog as US-only. Hits go to quarantine for human review, never to training. Never ingest from third-party mirrors (EverySpec, Scribd, personal faculty pages) because they may host restricted revisions. Record the screening tool version and result.
4. **PII.** Scan for names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, certificate and tail numbers tied to people, and medical details. This matters most for NTSB dockets, ASRS narratives (de-identified but still screened), theses (acknowledgments, author contact details) and Federal Register comment notices. Redact or drop. Keep public officials' names in official report authorship.
5. **Deduplication.** Use exact hashes on raw bytes and normalized text, then near-duplicate detection (MinHash/LSH on shingles). Expected duplicate pairs: NTRS↔DTIC (the same report under NASA and AD numbers), FAA ACs↔ROSA P↔DRS, eCFR↔Federal Register final rules, arXiv↔journal OA versions↔OpenAlex/CORE/S2ORC copies, JSBSim↔FlightGear aircraft models. Keep the copy with the strongest license evidence and the best text quality.
6. **Decontamination against eval sets.** Freeze eval and RL held-out items *before* building the final CPT mix. Remove CPT documents with n-gram overlap (e.g. 13-gram) against eval items and their source passages. Put canary strings in released eval files. Hold out by source and by time: for example, regulations and ACs revised after a cutoff date, or whole NTRS report series reserved for eval. Public aviation-exam material (FAA handbooks, knowledge-test Q&A) is a known contamination route for public benchmarks, so tag it.
7. **Provenance manifest.** Each document gets one append-only record: `doc_id`, `source` (Table 1 row), `source_url` (item), `landing_page_url`, `retrieval_datetime_utc`, `http_status`, `sha256_raw`, `sha256_text`, `extractor` + version, `license_as_stated`, `license_evidence_url` + `sha256` of the saved terms-page snapshot, `copyright_field`, `distribution_statement` (catalog and document), `export_screen` (result, tool version), `pii_screen` (result, tool version), `dedup_cluster`, `decontam_status`, `decision` (include, exclude or quarantine), `reviewer`, `notes`. Version the manifest with the data (e.g. a content-addressed store plus a signed manifest per release).
   - **DO-330 link.** If aero-foundation is ever used as, or inside, a development or verification tool in a DO-178C/ARP4754B project, a DO-330 tool qualification argument would need configuration-managed evidence of what the tool was built from. This manifest (sources, versions, hashes, licenses, exclusion decisions) is the start of that evidence trail, so it must be complete, reproducible and kept under configuration control from Phase 0. See [certification.md](certification.md).

## Recommended Phase 0 corpus

The priority order favors clean rights, direct relevance to the four target uses, and usefulness as tool-checkable RL material.

1. **NTRS public subset, including NACA.** Download bulk NDJSON *metadata* first, filter to `Distribution Limits = Public` and a US-Gov/public-use copyright field, then fetch PDFs through the OpenAPI links. This is the largest on-topic, rights-clean body of flight dynamics, handling qualities, flight control and wind-tunnel text. It also contains public-domain source data for classic benchmark aircraft (e.g. the F-16 data behind NASA TP-1538) that can feed aero-database RL tasks.
2. **14 CFR (eCFR Title 14) and Federal Register FAA documents (GovInfo bulk XML).** Public domain, structured and versioned. These are the base for requirements-traceability tasks (e.g. Part 25 Subpart B) and rationale tasks built from rulemaking preambles.
3. **ASSIST Distribution A flight documents.** MIL-F-8785C, MIL-F-83300, MIL-DTL-9490E, MIL-HDBK-516D and MIL-STD-882E. They give public-release handling-qualities and FCS requirements that can be turned into checkable criteria (e.g. short-period damping and frequency bounds evaluated on JSBSim-linearized models). Explicitly **exclude MIL-STD-1797A/B and MIL-HDBK-1797 (Dist D)**.
4. **Engineering tools and models for RL rewards.** JSBSim (61 aircraft), XFOIL, AVL, SU2, OpenVSP and python-control as reward oracles, plus parametric NACA geometry. Run only; no third-party data is redistributed. Use their permissively licensed docs and examples as CPT text, and decide whether to train on GPL/LGPL source.
5. **EASA CS-23/CS-25/AMC-20 (Easy Access Rules XML).** Reuse with attribution, including commercial reuse. Allows FAA↔EASA cross-traceability tasks.
6. **FAA Advisory Circulars, certification Orders and handbooks**, once the license column is confirmed with FAA. They are presumed US Gov works, but the fetched pages state nothing. Prioritize certification ACs (Part 25 flight test, software, systems) over pilot-training handbooks.
7. **NTSB reports and datasets** (public domain, after PII screening), for safety and requirements-gap eval items.
8. **License-filtered open literature.** arXiv CC0/CC BY papers in the relevant categories, found with OpenAlex (CC0 metadata) and its license field. Defer S2ORC/CORE full text until each item's license is checked.
