# Research landscape for aero-foundation

This survey covers prior work relevant to aero-foundation, an open-weights language model specialized for aerospace engineering. It is trained with continued pretraining and then with reinforcement learning whose rewards come from engineering tools that compute a checkable answer. The survey has four areas: (1) LLMs specialized for science and engineering domains, (2) LLMs that drive engineering tools, (3) reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) and its failure modes, and (4) LLMs applied to aerospace, aviation and control engineering. The emphasis is on 2025–2026 work, plus the earlier papers each area builds on.

**Method.** Candidates were found by web search. Every source below was fetched and checked on 2026-10-15. Each check confirmed that the title, the authors or organization, and the year matched the fetched page (an arXiv abstract page, a publisher or registry record, an official repository or project page, or an agency page). Descriptions are paraphrased. A number appears only if it was on the fetched page, which for most entries was the abstract alone. Where a page gave no results, the entry describes the work and reports no outcome. Results are as reported by the authors; this survey did not reproduce them. In the Year column, the year is the first public version unless noted.

## 1. Domain-specialized LLMs for science and engineering

Common patterns: start from a strong open base model (often a code model for math or EDA), continue pretraining on a curated domain corpus, then add instruction tuning or RL, and check general benchmarks for regressions. DeepSeekMath also belongs here (it was continued-pretrained from a code model on 120B math tokens). It is listed under area 3 because it introduced GRPO.

Reports state-of-the-art results on technical benchmarks without external tools, and about one third correct on 200+ undergraduate STEM problems. | Early evidence that continued training on technical/math text raises quantitative reasoning. | [Training a Scientific Reasoning Model for Chemistry (ether0)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17238) | Narayanan, Rodriques, White et al. | 2025 | Post-trains Mistral-Small-24B with RL on 640,730 experimentally grounded chemistry problems across 375 tasks, with no extra domain pretraining. Reports beating frontier models and human experts on molecular design tasks. | Shows that RL on checkable domain problems can specialize a mid-size open model without CPT. A baseline for testing whether aero CPT is needed at all. |
| [Domain Adapted Large Language Models for Additive Manufacturing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22017) | Pak, Barati Farimani | 2026 | Domain-adaptive pretraining plus instruction tuning of Gemma 3, Qwen 3 and Gemma 4 on about 50M tokens of open-access additive-manufacturing articles (text and vision). | [Fine-tuning LLMs for domain adaptation: training strategies, scaling, model merging...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03444) | Lu, Luu, Buehler | 2024 | Compares CPT, SFT, DPO and ORPO, then merges models, on Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral 7B for materials science. Proposes schedules that cause less forgetting than cosine. | The main methodological reference for limiting catastrophic forgetting during aero-foundation's CPT stage (replay ratio, LR schedule). |

## 2. LLMs used with engineering tools

The recurring loop is: the LLM writes an input deck or script, a solver runs it, and errors or outputs go back to the LLM. Reported success rates are self-reported and use benchmarks that differ between papers, so they are not comparable across rows.

| Work | Org / authors (short) | Year | What it does | Relevance to aero-foundation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761) | Schick, Dwivedi-Yu, Scialom et al. | 2023 | The model learns, self-supervised from a few demos per API, when to call tools (calculator, QA, search, translation, calendar) and how to use the results. | Foundational for training, rather than only prompting, tool-call behaviour into a model's weights. |
| [ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) | Yao, Zhao, Narasimhan, Cao et al. | 2022 | Interleaves reasoning traces with actions against an external environment. Reports gains on QA, fact checking and interactive decision tasks. | The basic agent loop that aero-foundation's tool-use rollouts (call simulator → read output → revise) follow. |
| [PAL: Program-aided Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10435) | Gao, Madaan, Neubig et al. | 2022 | The LLM writes a program and an interpreter computes the answer. Reports beating much larger chain-of-thought models on math and symbolic tasks. | Supports having the model write code that computes, rather than doing arithmetic in text, which is central to stability margins, trim and so on. |
| [ChemCrow: Augmenting large-language models with chemistry tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05376) | Bran, Cox, White, Schwaller et al. | 2023 | A GPT-4 agent with 18 expert chemistry tools. | [MechAgents: LLM multi-agent collaborations can solve mechanics problems...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08166) | Ni, Buehler | 2023 | Agent teams write, run and debug finite-element code for elasticity problems (varied BCs, geometry, constitutive laws), with planner, coder and critic roles. | Shows LLMs operating a structural solver end to end. On 315 cases it reports 82.1% execution success and a separate 68.12% physical-fidelity metric, including NACA0012 and supersonic-nozzle cases. | Separates "it runs" from "it is physically right", the distinction aero-foundation's rewards must make. | [CAD-Coder: Text-to-CAD Generation with Chain-of-Thought and Geometric Reward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19713) | Guan, Yu et al. | 2025 | SFT, then GRPO, with a reward combining Chamfer-distance geometry and output format, for CadQuery scripts. 110K text-code-shape triplets. NeurIPS 2025. | RL with a geometry-computed reward in mechanical CAD, close to using OpenVSP/geometry checks as rewards for aircraft layouts. |
| [OptiMUS: Optimization Modeling Using MIP Solvers and large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06116) | AhmadiTeshnizi, Gao, Udell | 2023 | Agent formulates MILPs from text, writes and debugs solver code, and checks solutions. | [Eureka: Human-Level Reward Design via Coding Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12931) | Ma, Fan, Anandkumar et al. | 2023 | The LLM writes reward-function code, refined by evolutionary search using simulator training results. Relevant if aero-foundation designs objectives for downstream controller tuning. |
| [FunSearch (Google DeepMind blog on the Nature 2023 paper)](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/funsearch-making-new-discoveries-in-mathematical-sciences-using-large-language-models/) | Romera-Paredes, Fawzi et al. (Google DeepMind) | 2023 | An LLM proposes programs, an automated evaluator scores them, and the best programs seed the next round. Reports new cap-set constructions and better online bin-packing heuristics. | Evaluator-in-the-loop search is an alternative or complement to RL training when an evaluator exists but little training data does. |
| [AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131) | Novikov, Balog, Kohli et al. | 2025 | Evolutionary coding agent: LLMs edit code and automated evaluators score candidates. Reports a 48-multiplication algorithm for 4×4 complex matrices and infrastructure gains. | Shows automated evaluators can drive search on real engineering artifacts (schedulers, circuits). Useful for gain-tuning or aero-database search experiments. |
| [SimuAgent: An LLM-Based Simulink Modeling Assistant Enhanced with RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05187) | Liang, Zhao | 2026 | Represents Simulink models as compact Python dicts with a local static-check testbed. Trains Qwen2.5-7B with "Reflection-GRPO". Releases SimuBench (5,300 multi-domain tasks including control). Reports beating standard RL baselines and few-shot GPT-4o on SimuBench. | Closest existing work to RL on a control-engineering toolchain. It also shows that compact model representations and cheap local checks make RL rollouts affordable. |

## 3. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)

Rows are grouped: (a) core methods and the question of whether RLVR adds capability, (b) rewards from execution, simulators and formal checkers, and (c) verifier failure modes and reward hacking. CodeV-R1 (area 1), CAD-Coder and SimuAgent (area 2) are further domain RLVR examples.

Gains look bounded by the base model, and distillation, unlike RLVR, added new patterns. Supports a broad task suite (control, aero, HQ) over a single narrow task. |
| [The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617) | Cui, Bai, Ding et al. | 2025 | Documents early policy-entropy collapse that caps performance, gives an empirical entropy-performance relation, and proposes Clip-Cov and KL-Cov to keep exploring. | Entropy collapse is a known failure mode to monitor in every aero RL run. | [BeamPERL: Parameter-Efficient RL with Verifiable Rewards ... | **(c) Verifier failure modes and reward hacking** | | | | |
| [Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior and the Risks of Promoting Obfuscation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926) | Baker, Madry, Zaremba, Pachocki et al. | 2025 | Documents reward hacking in agentic coding environments. | [From Accuracy to Robustness: A Study of Rule- and Model-based Verifiers in Mathematical Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22203) | Huang, Zeng, He et al. | 2025 | Rule-based verifiers reject correct answers written in other formats (false negatives that grow as the policy improves). | [Checking the Checkers: A Synthesis of Verifier Noise and Robustness in RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17824) | Gupta, Joshi | 2026 | Survey of 58 studies. Concludes that the structure of verifier errors matters more than their rate: exploitable false positives can stall or collapse training even at low rates, and errors shift as the policy improves. Proposes a reporting protocol. | Most current synthesis of verifier-robustness evidence. Its reporting protocol could be adopted for aero-foundation's reward suite. |

## 4. LLMs applied to aerospace, aviation, flight control and control engineering

This area is thinner than the others, and most work is from 2024–2026. Existing work falls into: control-engineering benchmarks and agents, spacecraft and drone operation in simulation, aviation-text NLP (safety reports, training, maintenance, requirements), and early design-generation agents. No work found trains a model with RL on rewards from flight-dynamics or handling-qualities tools (see Gaps).

| Work | Org / authors (short) | Year | What it does | Relevance to aero-foundation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

(UIUC, Michigan, Ai2, UCSD) | 2024 | 147 undergraduate control problems (from Schaum's and UIUC/Michigan courses) plus a 100-item multiple-choice variant ([project page](https://agi4engineering.github.io/LLM4Control/)). Expert-graded. Claude 3 Opus scored best of the three models tested. | A ready-made control-knowledge eval for regression testing. It is text-graded and does not execute designs. |

## Gaps and implications for aero-foundation

- **No published RL on flight-dynamics or handling-qualities rewards.** Searches found no work that RL-trains an LLM on rewards from flight simulators, linearized aircraft models, vortex-lattice codes or handling-qualities criteria. Control-design LLM work either evaluates without training (ControlBench, ControlAgent) or distills (CoDyControlBench). The nearest neighbours are BeamPERL (symbolic statics solver), RLVP (PDE residual rewards), SimuAgent (Simulink, RL) and the VAV study (simulator rollouts, a negative result). The gap is real, but the absence of prior work also means no evidence yet that it works.
- **No open aerospace-engineering CPT model or corpus.** AviationGPT targets operations text, aeroBERT models are encoders, and INDUS/EVE cover science and Earth observation. Recent small-corpus DAPT (about 50M tokens in additive manufacturing) and full-paper corpora (AstroSage) suggest a curated open aero-engineering corpus (NTRS reports, textbooks with permissive licenses, tool manuals, ASRS) is the first missing asset.
- **Plan for forgetting during CPT.** Use replay of general data with LR re-warming and re-decaying (Ibrahim et al.), track general benchmarks explicitly as ChipNeMo and ClimateGPT did, and keep model merging (Lu et al.) as a fallback to recover general and coding skill.
- **Treat "sharpening vs. new capability" as an open question to measure, not assume.** Yue et al. find RLVR bounded by the base model, while ProRL and Wen et al. find it can extend the boundary. Report pass@k curves, compare against distillation, and expect new aero knowledge to come mainly from CPT and distillation.
- **Run controls that rule out spurious gains.** Spurious Rewards and Dr. GRPO show that gains and length growth can appear without informative rewards, especially on Qwen bases. Every aero RL result should include a random-reward control and at least two base-model families.
- **Design rewards as hybrid and graded, and perturb them.** Follow RLVP: a hard gate (the tool runs, units and conventions are valid), then continuous error terms rather than pass/fail. Following BeamPERL and Isomorphic Perturbation Testing, score each answer on randomized or re-parameterized variants (moved CG, different flight condition, scaled plant) so templates and memorized outputs fail. Checkpoint selection should use held-out perturbation suites, since peak reward and peak robustness diverged in BeamPERL.
- **Harden the tool harness against the policy.** Agentic reward hacking (Baker et al., ImpossibleBench) includes editing tests and monkey-patching graders. Run simulators in a sandbox that the policy cannot write to. Compute rewards outside the rollout's reach. Seed "impossible" tasks to measure cheating rates. Use CoT monitoring for detection, but do not optimize against it directly.
- **Budget for verifier error structure, not only rate.** The Checking-the-Checkers synthesis and Huang et al. show that exploitable false positives are worse than random noise, and that rule-based checkers reject valid answers in other formats. Fuzz each aero verifier (sign conventions, unit systems, equivalent transfer-function forms) before training, and publish its false-positive and false-negative rates.
- **Monitor exploration.** Entropy collapse (Cui et al.) and the need for exploration in small-data RLVR (one-example RLVR) mean entropy should be logged per run, with Clip-Cov/KL-Cov ready as mitigations.
- **Evaluation is the cheapest first contribution.** Existing control and aviation evals are text-graded (ControlBench, Pre-Flight) or controller-success-only (CoDyControlBench). A benchmark that grades designs by running open tools (VLM aero coefficients, linear-model stability margins, handling-qualities criteria) would fill a gap even before any training. The tool-backed grading of FEABench and ChatCFD's run-vs-physically-correct split are models to follow.

## Method and limitations

- Searches were run and every cited page was fetched on 2026-10-15. Where a publisher page blocked automated access (MDPI, Nature), a Crossref metadata record or an official organization page was fetched instead, and the link points to that page.
- Most entries were checked against the abstract or landing page only. Numbers are those stated there. Full-paper methods (exact base models, reward formulas, hyperparameters) were not verified unless the page stated them. All results are author-reported and not independently reproduced.
- An affiliation appears in the Org / authors column only where a fetched page showed it (the cited page, a project page, a registry record, or for INDUS the arXiv HTML header). All other rows give author names only, because most arXiv abstract pages list no affiliations.
- Several 2026 entries are recent preprints that have not yet been peer-reviewed.
- This survey is not exhaustive. Search was in English. Paywalled journals (AIAA, IEEE, Elsevier) and non-English venues are underrepresented, and work in industry or behind export controls is likely missing.
